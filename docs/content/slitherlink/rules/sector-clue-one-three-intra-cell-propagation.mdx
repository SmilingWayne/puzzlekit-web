## What it does

When a numbered cell's clue is **1** or **3** and one corner sector is already
known to contain **exactly one line**, this rule decides the cell's two
**opposite** edges — the edges that do not belong to that sector.

- Clue **1**: both opposite edges are crossed out.
- Clue **3**: both opposite edges are marked as lines.

It does not change sector masks or vertex candidates.

## When it triggers

The rule inspects every cell with numeric clue **1** or **3**. For each corner
sector of that cell whose constraint has already been narrowed to **exactly one
line**, it looks at the two cell edges that are not part of that sector.

It acts when at least one of those opposite edges is still unknown. The sector
must already be at exactly-one-line from earlier sector reasoning; this rule
does not infer new sector masks.

If the sector is not exactly one line, or both opposite edges are already
decided, the case is skipped.

## Why it works

A corner sector has two edges. If sector reasoning has fixed that sector to
exactly one line, that line already accounts for part of the cell's clue count.

For clue **1**, the cell needs only one line in total. The sector's one line
already satisfies the clue, so the two remaining cell edges — the opposite pair
— cannot also be lines.

For clue **3**, the cell needs three lines. The sector contributes one, so the
opposite pair must supply the other two. Both must be lines.

Any other assignment would give the wrong total around the clue while still
violating the fixed sector count.
