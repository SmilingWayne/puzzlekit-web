## What it does

Inside/outside coloring labels each cell as inside or outside the loop. This
rule floods outward from every known inside cell through passages that are not
line edges, then colors every still-unknown cell that the flood never reaches as
outside.

A passage is any shared edge that is still unknown or crossed out. Line edges
block the flood. Existing outside cells and clue **3** cells also block the
flood and are never recolored by this rule.

One application may color many cells when several pockets are sealed off from
all inside sources at once.

## When it triggers

At least one cell must already be colored inside. The rule does nothing when no
inside anchor exists.

From those anchors, it walks to orthogonally adjacent cells across non-line
edges, skipping:

- Cells already colored outside.
- Clue **3** cells (whether or not they are colored yet).

Every remaining unknown cell that never joins the flood is colored outside. The
rule does not overwrite cells that already have a color.

No numbered clue is required beyond the implicit **3**-clue barrier behavior.

## Why it works

The loop separates the grid into an inside region and an outside region. Cells
in the same region can be reached from one another without crossing a line edge,
because a line is the border between opposite colors.

If a cell cannot be reached from any known inside cell without crossing a line,
it cannot lie on an inside-connected path. Every valid completion keeps such a
cell outside. Outside cells and **3** clues are hard barriers because they
already fix the parity on their side of the loop.
