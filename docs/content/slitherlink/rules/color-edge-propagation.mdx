## What it does

Inside/outside coloring treats every cell as either inside the loop or
outside it. This rule keeps that coloring aligned with edge decisions. It may
mark a shared edge as a line or as crossed out, decide boundary edges from a
colored border cell, or color an unknown neighbor once one side of an edge is
already known.

Concretely, it can:

- Mark a boundary edge as a line when the adjacent cell is inside, or crossed
  out when that cell is outside.
- Cross out an interior edge when both adjacent cells have the same color, or
  mark it as a line when their colors differ.
- Color the unknown side of an edge as inside or outside when the edge and the
  other adjacent cell are already decided.

## When it triggers

At least one unknown edge or unknown cell color must be reachable through these
patterns. No numbered clue is required.

**Color to edge**

- A boundary cell is colored inside or outside, and its outer boundary edge is
  still unknown.
- Two orthogonally adjacent cells are both colored, and the edge between them
  is still unknown.

**Edge to color**

- An edge is already a line or crossed out, exactly one adjacent cell is
  colored, and the other adjacent cell is still unknown.

The rule may apply several independent updates in one step when multiple edges
or cells satisfy these conditions at once.

## Why it works

Inside and outside are two regions separated by the loop. A line edge sits on
the border between them, so cells on opposite sides of a line must have
opposite colors. A crossed-out edge does not separate the regions, so cells on
both sides must share the same color.

A boundary edge has only one adjacent cell on the board. That outer side is
outside the puzzle, so an inside cell must meet the boundary with a line and an
outside cell must meet it with a crossed-out edge. Any update that violates
these relations would disagree with the loop's inside/outside partition.
