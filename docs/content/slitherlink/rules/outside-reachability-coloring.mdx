## What it does

Inside/outside coloring labels each cell as inside or outside the loop. This
rule floods inward from the exterior and from every known outside cell through
passages that are not line edges, then colors every still-unknown cell the flood
never reaches as inside.

The exterior seeds any border cell whose outer edge is still unknown or crossed
out. Existing outside cells are additional seeds. A passage is any shared edge
that is still unknown or crossed out; line edges block the flood. Existing
inside cells and clue **3** cells block the flood and are never recolored.

One application may color many cells when several enclosed pockets remain
unreachable from the exterior at once.

## When it triggers

At least one flood seed must exist:

- A cell already colored outside, or
- A border cell that can still reach off the board through a non-line boundary
  edge.

If every border cell is already walled off by lines on all outward edges and no
outside-colored cell is present, the rule does nothing.

From the seeds, it walks to orthogonally adjacent cells across non-line edges,
skipping inside cells and clue **3** cells. Every remaining unknown cell that
never joins the flood is colored inside. The rule does not overwrite cells that
already have a color.

## Why it works

The puzzle exterior is outside the loop. From any outside-colored cell or any
border opening, cells in the same outside region are reachable without crossing
a line, because lines separate opposite colors.

If an unknown cell cannot be reached from the exterior or from any known
outside cell without crossing a line, it sits in a sealed pocket. In every valid
completion that pocket is surrounded by loop edges, so its cells are inside.
Inside cells and **3** clues are hard barriers for the same parity reasons as in
the complementary inside flood.
