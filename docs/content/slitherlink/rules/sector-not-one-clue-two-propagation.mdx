## What it does

On a cell with clue **2**, when one corner sector cannot contain exactly one
line and the **diagonally opposite** corner sector already has a line on at
least one of its two edges, this rule crosses out every still-unknown edge in
the first sector.

It may blank both edges of the target corner sector in one application. It does
not draw new lines and does not change sector masks.

## When it triggers

The rule inspects every cell with numeric clue **2**. For each of the four
corner-sector pairs (northwest versus southeast, northeast versus southwest) it
checks:

1. The **target** corner sector's constraint forbids exactly one line (the mask
   does not allow a count of 1).
2. At least one edge of the **opposite** diagonal corner sector is already a
   line.
3. The target corner sector has no line yet on either of its two edges.
4. At least one target-sector edge is still unknown.

All four diagonal orientations are tried independently. If the target sector
already has a line, the opposite sector has no line, the target sector still
allows exactly one line, or every target edge is already decided, that
orientation is skipped.

## Why it works

Clue **2** needs exactly two lines on the four cell edges. The two corner
sectors on one diagonal each contribute their own pair of edges; the opposite
edges of the cell form the other diagonal pair.

If the opposite diagonal sector already uses a line, part of the clue count is
committed away from the target sector's corner. If the target sector **cannot**
have exactly one line, its only legal totals are zero or two lines. With the
opposite diagonal already contributing a line toward the cell total, giving the
target sector any line would push the cell over two lines or force the target
sector into the forbidden count of one when combined with the remaining cell
edges.

So every surviving solution leaves both target-sector edges crossed out. Marking
them blank is safe and may enable later sector or clue propagation.
