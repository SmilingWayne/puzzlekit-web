## What it does

Each cell corner has a corner sector with two edges. The two cells that sit
just outside that corner — one above or below and one left or right — are the
sector’s outside neighbors. This rule keeps sector line-count constraints and
those neighbors’ inside/outside colors aligned.

It may:

- Color an unknown outside neighbor once the sector already requires its two
  neighbors to match or to differ, and the other neighbor’s color is known.
- Narrow a sector’s allowed line count when both outside neighbors are already
  colored: matching colors forbid exactly one sector line, and opposite colors
  require exactly one.

Off-board outside-neighbor directions count as outside. Sector masks that already
forbid a count of one line — including masks that fix zero or two lines — are
treated like a “not exactly one” constraint for the color link. One
application may update several cells and sectors independently.

## When it triggers

At least one corner sector must satisfy one of these patterns:

**Sector mask to color**

- The sector is narrowed to exactly one line, or it forbids exactly one line
  (including masks that already fix zero or two lines).
- Exactly one in-bounds outside neighbor is still unknown; the other neighbor
  is known, or is off-board and treated as outside.
- The unknown neighbor receives the color forced by the sector relation.

**Color to sector mask**

- Both outside neighbors have known colors, or one is off-board and treated as
  outside.
- The sector is not already narrowed to only one line or to forbid one line.
- Matching neighbor colors narrow the sector so it cannot have exactly one
  line; opposite colors narrow it to exactly one line.

The rule does not act when both outside neighbors are unknown and in bounds, when
both are already colored and the sector is already in one of the narrowed
states, or when a proposed mask intersection would be empty. It does not
overwrite an existing cell color.

## Why it works

A corner sector has only two edges, so its line count is 0, 1, or 2. The two
outside neighbors lie on opposite sides of those edges. If they have opposite
inside/outside colors, the loop border runs between them and exactly one sector
edge must be a line — so the sector count is 1. If they share a color, both
sector edges must be lines or both must be crosses, so the sector cannot end
with exactly one line.

Once a sector is narrowed to one of those relations, a single unknown neighbor
has only one compatible color. Conversely, once both neighbor colors are fixed,
only one sector mask remains compatible with the corner geometry.
