## What it does

Inside/outside coloring labels each cell as inside or outside the loop. A
numbered clue counts how many of its four side edges are lines. This rule uses
those two views together: it may color the clue cell itself, or color unknown
orthogonal neighbors once enough neighboring inside/outside cells are already
known.

Directions off the board count as outside neighbors. One application may update
several cells when multiple numbered clues qualify at once.

## When it triggers

At least one numbered clue cell must participate, and at least one unknown cell
color must follow from one of these patterns:

**Contradiction on the clue cell**

- The clue is smaller than the current inside-neighbor count, or larger than
  `4 − outside-neighbor count`. The clue cell is colored inside.
- The clue is smaller than the outside-neighbor count, or larger than
  `4 − inside-neighbor count`. The clue cell is colored outside.

**Clue 2 with two known outside neighbors**

- Exactly two orthogonal neighbors are already outside. Every remaining unknown
  neighbor is colored inside.

**Clue 2 with two known inside neighbors**

- Exactly two orthogonal neighbors are already inside. Every remaining unknown
  neighbor is colored outside.

**Clue cell already colored**

- The clue cell is inside and already has as many outside neighbors as the clue
  value. Every remaining unknown neighbor is inside.
- The clue cell is outside and already has as many inside neighbors as the clue
  value. Every remaining unknown neighbor is outside.
- The clue cell is outside and the outside-neighbor count fixes how many more
  inside neighbors are still required. Every remaining unknown neighbor is
  inside.
- The clue cell is inside and the inside-neighbor count fixes how many more
  outside neighbors are still required. Every remaining unknown neighbor is
  outside.

Prior edge decisions are not required, but any already-colored neighbors are
used in the counts. The rule does not overwrite an existing cell color.

## Why it works

A clue counts line edges around its cell. In a valid coloring, an outside
neighbor sits across a crossed-out edge from the clue side that contributes no
line, while an inside neighbor sits across a line edge. So the clue value limits
how many neighbors can be outside versus inside.

If the known neighbor counts already make the clue impossible for one side, the
clue cell must be on the other side. For clue 2, two outside neighbors already
use both non-line sides, so every remaining side must be a line and its neighbor
inside — and the symmetric argument applies when two inside neighbors are known.
When the clue cell’s own color is fixed, the same bookkeeping forces every
still-unknown neighbor once the matching count is already saturated or the
remaining requirement is fixed.

Any other assignment would make the clue disagree with the loop’s
inside/outside partition.
