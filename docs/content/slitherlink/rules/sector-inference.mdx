## What it does

Each numbered cell has four corner sectors. A corner sector is the pair of edges
that meet at that cell corner. This rule narrows how many of those two edges may
be lines, without drawing or crossing them out directly.

One application may update several sectors. The allowed counts it can enforce
include:

- exactly zero, one, or two sector lines;
- forbidding exactly one sector line;
- forbidding zero sector lines;
- forbidding two sector lines.

## When it triggers

The rule inspects every corner sector. It acts when existing board evidence
narrows the sector below its current mask. No prior sector mask is required;
boundary geometry alone can start the deduction.

Evidence comes from three local sources, combined for that sector:

**Sector edges already decided**

- If both sector edges are decided, the sector is narrowed to that exact line
  count.

**Vertex flow at the shared corner vertex**

- A board corner with only the two sector edges incident: the sector cannot have
  exactly one line.
- Exactly one non-sector edge at the vertex is crossed out: the sector cannot
  have exactly one line.
- Exactly one non-sector edge is a line, and every remaining unknown edge at the
  vertex lies in this sector: the sector must have exactly one line.
- Two non-sector edges are lines: the sector must have zero lines.
- Two non-sector edges are crossed out: the sector cannot have exactly one line.
- One sector edge is crossed out: the sector cannot have two lines.

**Numbered clue on the cell**

- If the cell's decided edge lines already equal the clue, the sector is
  narrowed to its own decided line count.
- Clue 3 with both diagonal (non-sector) edges already lines: the sector must
  have exactly one line.
- Clue 2 with one diagonal line and one diagonal cross: the sector must have
  exactly one line.
- Clue 1 with both diagonal edges crossed out: the sector must have exactly one
  line.
- Clue 3, or one sector edge already a line: the sector cannot have zero lines.
- Clue 1: the sector cannot have two lines.

The rule only tightens masks. It never relaxes a sector that is already more
specific, and it skips any intersection that would become empty.

## Why it works

A corner sector has only two edges, so its line count is 0, 1, or 2. Vertex
degree limits how those two edges can combine with the other edges at the same
corner vertex: a valid loop gives every used vertex degree two, so the sector
edges and the non-sector edges at that vertex must agree on how many lines
remain. Board corners and single-edge vertices rule out a lone sector line when
the geometry cannot support it.

Clue arithmetic applies the same idea at the cell level. Once enough edges
around the clue are fixed — including the two sector edges and the two diagonal
edges opposite them — only one sector line count stays compatible with the clue
and with forbidden totals.
